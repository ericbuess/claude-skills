# Historical Jesus and Resurrection Evidence

Bayesian analysis of historical evidence for Jesus' life, death, and resurrection.

## Historical Method

### Criteria of Authenticity

Historians use these to assess Gospel reliability:

1. **Multiple Attestation**: Reported in multiple independent sources
2. **Embarrassment**: Details that would embarrass early church (unlikely to invent)
3. **Dissimilarity**: Differs from both Judaism and early Christianity (not influenced by either)
4. **Coherence**: Fits with established authentic material
5. **Enemy Attestation**: Hostile sources confirm (stronger than friendly sources)

---

## Minimal Facts Approach (Gary Habermas)

**Strategy**: Only use facts accepted by ~75% of scholars (including skeptics)

**Core Facts**:

1. **Jesus died by crucifixion** (95%+ accept)
   - Multiple attestation: All 4 Gospels, Paul, Josephus, Tacitus
   - Embarrassment: Crucifixion was shameful death
   - Enemy attestation: Josephus, Talmud confirm crucifixion

2. **Disciples believed Jesus appeared to them** (90%+ accept)
   - 1 Cor 15:3-7 (early creed, Paul met eyewitnesses)
   - Acts, Gospels, church history
   - Even skeptics grant: *Something* happened that convinced disciples

3. **Paul converted due to experience of risen Jesus** (90%+ accept)
   - Self-attested in letters (Galatians, 1 Corinthians)
   - Radical transformation (persecutor → apostle)
   - No motive to fabricate (suffered for it)

4. **James (skeptical brother) converted** (80%+ accept)
   - John 7:5: Brothers didn't believe during ministry
   - 1 Cor 15:7: Appearance to James
   - Became church leader, martyred

5. **Empty tomb** (50-75% accept—debated)
   - Women witnesses (embarrassment—women's testimony not valued)
   - Enemy attestation: Matthew 28 implies Jewish leaders acknowledged empty tomb (claimed disciples stole body)
   - Early Jerusalem church (couldn't proclaim resurrection if body still in tomb)

## Evidence Evaluation (Using 5-Tier Hierarchy)

### 1 Corinthians 15:3-7 (Early Creed)

**Text**:
> "For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures, and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve. Then he appeared to more than five hundred brothers at one time, most of whom are still alive, though some have fallen asleep. Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles."

**Evidence Tier**: **Testimonial (High End: 70-80%)**

**Strengths**:
- **Early**: Paul writing ~55 AD, creed earlier (~35-40 AD, within 5-10 years of event)
- **Eyewitness**: Paul met Peter, James (Gal 1:18-19)
- **Multiple witnesses**: 500+, many still alive (inviting verification)
- **No motive to lie**: Disciples suffered persecution, martyrdom

**Weaknesses**:
- **Not fully independent**: Synoptic problem (Matthew, Luke use Mark)
- **No hostile witnesses**: All early sources are believers
- **Legendary development possible**: 20 years still allows embellishment

**Independence Check**: 1 Cor 15 predates Gospels, independent source. But Gospels share material (synoptic), so ~2-3 independent traditions, not 5.

---

### Empty Tomb

**Evidence Tier**: **Testimonial (Medium: 55-70%)**

**Strengths**:
- **Women witnesses** (Mary Magdalene, etc.): Embarrassment criterion (women's testimony not valued in 1st century, unlikely invented detail)
- **Enemy attestation**: Matthew 28:11-15 implies Jewish leaders acknowledged tomb was empty (claimed disciples stole body)
- **Early proclamation in Jerusalem**: If body still in tomb, resurrection claim would be easily disproven

**Weaknesses**:
- **Not in 1 Cor 15**: Earliest creed doesn't mention empty tomb (argument from silence)
- **Alternative explanations**: Stolen body, wrong tomb, spiritual resurrection (no physical body)

**Bayesian**: P(Empty Tomb | Resurrection) = 90-95%. P(Empty Tomb | Legend) = 20-40%. Modest evidence for resurrection.

---

### Post-Resurrection Appearances

**Evidence Tier**: **Testimonial (High: 65-80%)**

**Strengths**:
- **Multiple independent sources**: 1 Cor 15, Gospels, Acts
- **Group appearances**: Harder to explain as hallucination (individuals hallucinate, groups less so)
- **Variety**: Different people, places, times (Peter, twelve, James, 500, Paul on Damascus road)

**Weaknesses**:
- **Hallucination possible**: Grief hallucinations common
- **Legendary development**: Stories could grow over time
- **Expectation bias**: Disciples expected Messiah, might see what they wanted

## Bayesian Analysis: Did Jesus Rise from the Dead?

### Hypotheses

1. **H1: Resurrection** (Jesus rose bodily)
2. **H2: Legend** (Story developed over decades, didn't happen)
3. **H3: Hallucination** (Disciples experienced grief hallucinations)
4. **H4: Swoon** (Jesus didn't actually die, revived in tomb)

### Priors P(H)

**H1: Resurrection**:
- **Reference class**: All humans who've ever died (~100 billion)
- **Base rate**: 0 resurrections (excluding Jesus)
- **But**: If Jesus is God incarnate, reference class changes (not just another human)
- **Prior**: 15-30% (low due to no other resurrections, higher if open to Jesus' divinity)

**H2: Legend**:
- **Reference class**: Religious movements often develop legends (cargo cults, UFO religions)
- **Prior**: 40-50%

**H3: Hallucination**:
- **Reference class**: Grief hallucinations common
- **Prior**: 30-40%

**H4: Swoon**:
- **Rejected by nearly all scholars** (crucifixion was fatal, Roman soldiers expert executioners)
- **Prior**: <5%

### Likelihoods P(E|H)

| Evidence | P(E\|Resurrection) | P(E\|Legend) | P(E\|Hallucination) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early Creed (1 Cor 15) | 95% | 30% | 60% |
| Empty Tomb | 90% | 30% | 50% |
| Group Appearances | 95% | 20% | 40% |
| James' Conversion | 90% | 30% | 60% |
| Paul's Conversion | 90% | 20% | 50% |

**Combined Likelihood** (assuming independence—debatable):
- P(All E | Resurrection) = 0.95 × 0.90 × 0.95 × 0.90 × 0.90 ≈ **62%**
- P(All E | Legend) = 0.30 × 0.30 × 0.20 × 0.30 × 0.20 ≈ **0.1%**
- P(All E | Hallucination) = 0.60 × 0.50 × 0.40 × 0.60 × 0.50 ≈ **3.6%**

### Posterior Calculation

**Using Bayes' Theorem**:

```
P(Resurrection | All E) = (0.62 × 0.20) / (0.62 × 0.20 + 0.001 × 0.45 + 0.036 × 0.35)
                        = 0.124 / (0.124 + 0.00045 + 0.0126)
                        = 0.124 / 0.137
                        ≈ 90%
```

**Sensitivity Analysis**:

- **Low Prior (15%)**: Posterior ≈ 85%
- **Medium Prior (25%)**: Posterior ≈ 92%
- **High Prior (35%)**: Posterior ≈ 95%

**Interpretation**: Evidence significantly raises credence for resurrection. But posterior depends heavily on prior (whether you're open to miracles).

## Alternative Explanations Evaluated

### Legend Development

**Claim**: Story grew over decades, didn't happen

**Strengths**:
- Religions often develop legends (Joseph Smith's golden plates)
- Psychological: People see what they expect

**Weaknesses**:
- **Too early**: 1 Cor 15 creed within 5-10 years (insufficient time for legend)
- **Eyewitnesses alive**: Paul says 500 witnesses still living (inviting verification)
- **Martyrdom**: Disciples died for belief (people don't die for known lies)

**Bayesian**: P(All E | Legend) ≈ 0.1% (very low)

---

### Hallucination

**Claim**: Disciples experienced grief hallucinations

**Strengths**:
- Grief hallucinations common (seeing deceased loved ones)
- Psychological mechanism well-documented

**Weaknesses**:
- **Group appearances**: 500 people don't share same hallucination
- **Variety**: Different people, times, places (hallucinations typically individual, brief)
- **Empty tomb**: Hallucination doesn't explain missing body
- **Skeptics converted**: James, Paul not grieving (Paul was persecutor), yet experienced appearances

**Bayesian**: P(All E | Hallucination) ≈ 3.6% (low, but higher than legend)

---

### Stolen Body / Conspiracy

**Claim**: Disciples stole body, lied about resurrection

**Strengths**:
- Matthew 28 records Jewish leaders making this claim

**Weaknesses**:
- **Martyrdom**: Disciples died for belief (people don't die for known lies)
- **Psychological implausibility**: Maintaining conspiracy for decades, all refusing to recant under torture
- **Paul's conversion**: Persecutor wouldn't join conspiracy

**Rejected by nearly all scholars** (including skeptics like Bart Ehrman)

---

## Expert Opinion

**Conservative Scholars** (Craig, Wright, Habermas): Resurrection best explanation, 80-95% confidence

**Liberal Scholars** (Crossan, Lüdemann): Something happened (visions/hallucinations?), but not bodily resurrection, 10-30% for resurrection

**Moderate Scholars** (Ehrman, Allison): Genuinely uncertain, 20-50%

**Epistemic Humility**: Significant expert disagreement → reduce confidence

---

## Summary Bayesian Assessment

**Posterior Credence for Resurrection**:
- **If skeptical prior** (10-15%): 60-75%
- **If moderate prior** (20-25%): 75-85%
- **If open prior** (30-40%): 85-95%

**Key Takeaway**: Evidence is substantial, but prior probability of miracles matters enormously. If you're open to supernatural, evidence is compelling. If you're skeptical, evidence is surprising but not conclusive.
