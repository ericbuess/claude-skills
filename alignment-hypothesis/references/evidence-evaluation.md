# Evidence Evaluation Framework

Comprehensive guide for grading theological and philosophical evidence.

## Five-Tier Evidence Hierarchy

### Tier 1: DEDUCTIVE (95-99% confidence if sound)

**Definition**: Valid logical argument + true premises → necessarily true conclusion

**Structure**:
```
1. All A are B (premise 1 - must be true)
2. C is A (premise 2 - must be true)
3. Therefore, C is B (conclusion - necessarily follows)
```

**Theological Examples**:
- Ontological Argument (Anselm): "God is that than which nothing greater can be conceived. Existence is greater than non-existence. Therefore, God must exist."
  - **Problem**: Premise 2 is contested (Kant's objection: existence isn't a predicate)
  - **Confidence**: 10-30% (validity yes, soundness debated)

- Kalam Cosmological Argument: "Everything that begins to exist has a cause. The universe began to exist. Therefore, the universe has a cause."
  - **Problem**: Is "cause" = "God"? Requires additional argument.
  - **Confidence**: 60-80% that universe has a cause, 30-50% that cause is God

**How to Evaluate**:
1. Check **validity**: Does conclusion follow from premises?
2. Check **soundness**: Are premises actually true?
3. Assess each premise independently

**Confidence Ceiling**: Even sound deductive arguments rarely reach 100% because premises can be challenged.

---

### Tier 2: STRONG INDUCTIVE (75-90%)

**Definition**: Pattern observed across large, representative sample → generalization likely true

**Requirements**:
- Large sample size (n > 100 for statistical significance)
- Representative (not cherry-picked)
- Controlled for confounding variables

**Theological Examples**:
- Fine-Tuning Constants: Measured precisely across multiple independent methods (gravitational constant, cosmological constant, etc.)
  - **Confidence**: 90-95% that constants are fine-tuned for life
  - **But**: Doesn't prove God (multiverse alternative)

- Near-Death Experiences (NDEs): Thousands of reports with similar features (tunnel, light, deceased relatives)
  - **Problem**: Not controlled (selection bias—only survivors report), cultural variations
  - **Confidence**: 40-60% (interesting but not decisive evidence for afterlife)

**How to Evaluate**:
1. Sample size: How many observations?
2. Representativeness: Any selection bias?
3. Independence: Are observations truly independent?
4. Controls: Were alternative explanations ruled out?

---

### Tier 3: ABDUCTIVE (60-80%)

**Definition**: Inference to Best Explanation—hypothesis that best explains the data

**Structure**:
1. Observe data D
2. Compare hypotheses H1, H2, H3...
3. Rank by explanatory virtues (scope, simplicity, coherence, etc.)
4. Conclude: H1 is most likely true

**Theological Examples**:
- Fine-Tuning + Consciousness + Moral Realism → Best explained by theism? Or multiverse + emergent properties?
  - **Theism**: Explains all three well
  - **Naturalism + Multiverse**: Explains fine-tuning, struggles with consciousness/morals
  - **Confidence**: 50-70% for theism depending on priors

**Explanatory Virtues** (weighted):
1. **Scope** (30%): How much data explained?
2. **Precision** (20%): Specific predictions?
3. **Coherence** (20%): Fits with established knowledge?
4. **Simplicity** (15%): Fewest ad-hoc assumptions (Occam's Razor)?
5. **Fecundity** (10%): New predictions/insights?
6. **Conservatism** (5%): Minimal revision of prior beliefs?

**How to Evaluate**:
1. List competing explanations
2. Score each on 6 criteria
3. Calculate weighted average
4. Provide range based on uncertainty

**Confidence Ceiling**: 60-80% (multiple viable explanations usually exist)

---

### Tier 4: TESTIMONIAL (40-80%, highly variable)

**Definition**: Evidence based on witness reports

**Depends on**:
1. **Witness Credibility**: Expertise, track record, honesty
2. **Corroboration**: Multiple independent witnesses?
3. **Motivation**: Any reason to lie or exaggerate?
4. **Consistency**: Do reports agree on key details?
5. **Contemporaneity**: How long after event were reports made?

**Theological Examples**:
- **Resurrection** (High End: 60-80%):
  - Multiple witnesses (500+ according to Paul)
  - Early reports (1 Cor 15 written ~55 AD, 20-25 years after event)
  - Disciples' martyrdom (no motive to lie)
  - BUT: No hostile witnesses, reports not fully independent (synoptic problem)

- **Miracles at Lourdes** (Mid Range: 40-60%):
  - Medical committee investigates
  - Some cases well-documented
  - BUT: Selection bias (millions pray, handful healed), no controlled studies

- **Alien Abductions** (Low End: 10-30%):
  - Thousands of reports
  - BUT: Sleep paralysis better explanation, no physical evidence, motivated reasoning

**How to Evaluate**:
1. Witness expertise: Are they qualified to judge what they saw?
2. Corroboration: Multiple independent sources?
3. Motive: Any incentive to fabricate?
4. Consistency: Do accounts align?
5. Alternative explanations: Hallucination? Legend? Misinterpretation?

**Confidence Range**: 40-80% for strong testimony, 10-30% for weak

---

### Tier 5: EXPERIENTIAL (30-90% for self, 20-50% for others)

**Definition**: Subjective experience (mystical, spiritual, phenomenological)

**Examples**:
- "I felt God's presence during prayer"
- "I experienced oneness with the universe during meditation"
- "I sense my deceased grandmother's spirit"

**For the Person Experiencing**:
- **High confidence** (70-90%): "I definitely experienced *something*"
- **Lower confidence** (30-60%): "Was it God? Or neurological phenomenon?"

**For External Observers**:
- **Low confidence** (20-50%): Hard to verify, subjective
- Principle of Charity: Don't dismiss, but don't treat as decisive evidence

**How to Evaluate**:
1. **Consistency**: Does experience align with expectations (confirmation bias)?
2. **Alternative Explanations**: Neuroscience (temporal lobe activity), psychology (pareidolia, apophenia)?
3. **Replicability**: Can others induce similar experiences (meditation, psychedelics)?
4. **Predictive Power**: Does experience lead to verifiable predictions?

**Confidence Ceiling**: 30-90% for experiencer, 20-50% for others

## Logical Fallacies Catalog

### Ad Hominem
Attacking the person instead of the argument.

**Example**: "You're an atheist, so your critique of the cosmological argument is biased."
**Counter**: Evaluate the argument's merit independently of who makes it.

### Straw Man
Misrepresenting opponent's position to make it easier to attack.

**Example**: "Atheists believe everything came from nothing for no reason."
**Counter**: Most atheists accept Big Bang (something from prior state), not ex nihilo creation.

### False Dilemma
Presenting only two options when more exist.

**Example**: "Either the Bible is 100% inerrant, or it's completely unreliable."
**Counter**: Third option—Bible is historically valuable but contains errors/contradictions.

### Appeal to Authority
Citing expert opinion as conclusive proof.

**Example**: "Stephen Hawking said philosophy is dead, so we should only trust science."
**Counter**: Hawking is expert in physics, not philosophy. Argument from authority is weak.

### Circular Reasoning (Begging the Question)
Assuming what you're trying to prove.

**Example**: "The Bible is God's word. How do I know? The Bible says so."
**Counter**: Requires independent justification for Bible's divine authorship.

### Equivocation
Using a word with multiple meanings interchangeably.

**Example**: "God is love. Love exists. Therefore, God exists."
**Counter**: "Love" as emotion vs. "God" as personal being are different concepts.

### Appeal to Ignorance
Arguing that because something hasn't been disproven, it must be true.

**Example**: "No one has proven God doesn't exist, so God must exist."
**Counter**: Burden of proof is on the one making the positive claim.

### Post Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc
Assuming A caused B just because A preceded B.

**Example**: "I prayed for healing, then got better. Therefore, prayer caused healing."
**Counter**: Correlation ≠ causation. Need controlled study.

### Hasty Generalization
Drawing broad conclusion from small sample.

**Example**: "I know three atheists who are immoral, so atheism leads to immorality."
**Counter**: Three examples insufficient. Need representative sample.

### Red Herring
Introducing irrelevant topic to distract from main argument.

**Example**: "You question God's goodness? What about Stalin's atheist regime?"
**Counter**: Stalin's atrocities don't address whether God is good.

## Grading Rubric

For any piece of evidence:

### Step 1: Assign Tier

- **Deductive**: Is it a valid logical argument? Are premises true?
- **Inductive**: Is it based on large, representative sample?
- **Abductive**: Is it best explanation among alternatives?
- **Testimonial**: Is it based on witness reports?
- **Experiential**: Is it subjective experience?

### Step 2: Assess Quality Within Tier

**High Quality** (upper end of range):
- Deductive: Premises well-established, logic airtight
- Inductive: Large n, controlled, independent
- Abductive: Clearly best explanation, few viable alternatives
- Testimonial: Multiple independent credible witnesses, no motive to lie
- Experiential: Consistent, replicable, predictive

**Low Quality** (lower end of range):
- Deductive: Contested premises, questionable logic
- Inductive: Small sample, selection bias, confounds
- Abductive: Multiple viable alternatives, limited scope
- Testimonial: Single source, motivated reasoning, legendary development possible
- Experiential: Easily explained naturalistically, confirmation bias

### Step 3: Check for Fallacies

Does argument commit any logical fallacies? If yes, reduce confidence by 10-30%.

### Step 4: Independence Check

Are multiple pieces of evidence truly independent? Or do they share a common source?

**Example**: Four Gospels
- Matthew, Luke depend on Mark (synoptic problem)
- John independent
- **Effective**: ~2 independent sources, not 4

### Step 5: Assign Confidence Range

Combine tier + quality + fallacy check + independence → Final confidence.

**Example**: Resurrection Testimony
- **Tier**: Testimonial (40-80%)
- **Quality**: Multiple early witnesses, martyrdom (high end: 70-80%)
- **Fallacies**: None obvious
- **Independence**: Partial (synoptic problem reduces to 60-75%)
- **Final**: 60-75%

## Confidence Level Calibration

To avoid overconfidence:

### For Single Piece of Evidence

- **Deductive (sound)**: 95-99%
- **Strong Inductive**: 75-90%
- **Abductive (clear best)**: 60-80%
- **Strong Testimonial**: 60-75%
- **Weak Testimonial**: 40-55%
- **Experiential (self)**: 50-80%
- **Experiential (other)**: 20-40%

### For Combined Evidence

If evidence pieces are **independent**, multiply probabilities.

**Example**: Two independent witnesses
- Each: 70% reliable
- Combined: 1 - (0.30 × 0.30) = 91%

**BUT**: Check independence! If witnesses influenced each other, don't multiply.

### Expert Disagreement Adjustments

- Modest disagreement (60/40): -10%
- Significant disagreement (70/30): -20%
- Deep disagreement (80/20): -30%

## Summary Checklist

For every piece of evidence:

- [ ] **Assign Tier**: Deductive / Inductive / Abductive / Testimonial / Experiential
- [ ] **Assess Quality**: High / Medium / Low within tier
- [ ] **Check Fallacies**: Ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, etc.
- [ ] **Verify Independence**: Are multiple sources truly independent?
- [ ] **Expert Opinion**: Do experts agree? If not, adjust confidence down.
- [ ] **Provide Range**: "60-75%", not "67%"
- [ ] **Justify**: Explain why this tier and this confidence level

**Goal**: Rigorous, transparent evaluation that acknowledges uncertainty and invites critique.
