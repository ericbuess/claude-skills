# Worldview Landscape: Steel-Manning Major Positions

Comprehensive overview of major worldviews on ultimate questions—presented in **strongest possible form** (steel-manned).

## Principle: No Straw Men

Each position below is presented **as a thoughtful proponent would present it**, not as an opponent would caricature it.

---

## 1. CLASSICAL THEISM (Christian, Jewish, Islamic)

### Core Claims

- **One God** who is:
  - **Omnipotent** (all-powerful)
  - **Omniscient** (all-knowing)
  - **Omnibenevolent** (all-good)
  - **Personal** (relational, conscious)
  - **Eternal** (outside time)
  - **Creator** (brought universe into existence ex nihilo)

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Cosmological**: Everything contingent has a cause. Universe is contingent. Therefore, necessary being (God) exists.
2. **Fine-Tuning**: Physical constants finely tuned for life → Design more plausible than chance.
3. **Moral**: Objective moral values exist → Require transcendent moral lawgiver.
4. **Consciousness**: Hard problem of consciousness → Best explained by immaterial soul created by God.
5. **Religious Experience**: Billions report experiencing God → Testimony not easily dismissed.

### Strongest Version

**Richard Swinburne** (Oxford): "Theism is simplest explanation for fine-tuning, consciousness, moral realism, and religious experience. One eternal mind is ontologically simpler than infinite material universes or brute facts."

### Main Challenges

1. **Problem of Evil**: Why does all-good, all-powerful God permit suffering?
2. **Divine Hiddenness**: Why isn't God more obvious?
3. **Religious Diversity**: Why do equally sincere seekers arrive at different religions?
4. **Parsimony**: Is God + universe simpler than just universe?

### Bayesian Priors

- **Simple version**: 20-40% (complexity penalty from extra entity)
- **After fine-tuning**: 50-70% (evidence boosts credence)
- **After evil**: 30-50% (theodicy challenges lower credence)

## 2. DEISM

### Core Claims

- **God exists** and created universe
- **But**: God doesn't intervene (no miracles, no answered prayers, no revelation)
- Universe runs on natural laws

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Fine-Tuning**: Constants set by designer
2. **Cosmological**: First cause required
3. **BUT**: No need for ongoing intervention—universe self-sufficient

### Strongest Version

**Enlightenment Deism** (Thomas Paine, Voltaire): "A perfectly rational God would create perfect natural laws, not intervene arbitrarily. Miracles would be admitting imperfect design."

### Main Challenges

1. **Why create?**: If God doesn't care to intervene, why create at all?
2. **Moral guidance**: How do we know right from wrong without revelation?
3. **Same arguments as theism**: Fine-tuning, first cause—but loses explanatory power for religious experience, moral realism

### Bayesian Priors

- **Simple version**: 10-20% (simpler than classical theism, but less explanatory)

---

## 3. PANTHEISM (Spinoza, some Hinduism)

### Core Claims

- **God = Universe** (not separate from creation)
- **Everything is divine**
- **No personal God**, but ultimate reality is sacred/conscious

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Parsimony**: No need for separate creator—universe itself is divine
2. **Religious Experience**: Mystical experiences of "oneness" with universe
3. **Avoids Theodicy**: No separate God to blame for evil—evil is part of divine whole

### Strongest Version

**Spinoza**: "God or Nature (Deus sive Natura)—one substance with infinite attributes. Traditional theism anthropomorphizes God; pantheism recognizes God as ultimate reality itself."

### Main Challenges

1. **Loses Personality**: Can't pray to, relate to impersonal God
2. **Moral Ambiguity**: If everything is divine, is evil also divine?
3. **Explanatory Power**: Doesn't explain fine-tuning better than naturalism

### Bayesian Priors

- **Simple version**: 5-15% (less explanatory than classical theism or naturalism)

---

## 4. PANENTHEISM (Process Theology)

### Core Claims

- **God contains universe**, but universe not identical to God
- **God evolves with creation** (not static)
- **God is good but not omnipotent** (limited power)

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Theodicy**: God doesn't cause suffering—God suffers with us, but can't prevent all evil
2. **Divine Immanence**: God intimately involved in creation
3. **Coherence**: Avoids contradictions of classical theism (omnipotence + omniben

olence + evil)

### Strongest Version

**Alfred North Whitehead, Charles Hartshorne**: "God persuades but doesn't coerce. Free will requires limits on God's power. This solves problem of evil."

### Main Challenges

1. **Not Omnipotent**: Is limited God worthy of worship?
2. **Biblical Tension**: Bible presents God as all-powerful
3. **Explanatory Power**: Less clear on fine-tuning (if God can't control constants, who/what did?)

### Bayesian Priors

- **Simple version**: 5-10% (niche position, less parsimonious than classical theism or naturalism)

---

## 5. POLYTHEISM (Ancient Greek, Hindu, Norse)

### Core Claims

- **Multiple gods** with limited domains (sea, war, harvest, etc.)
- **Not omnipotent individually**
- **Finite, relational beings**

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Religious Experience**: Different cultures experience different deities
2. **Diversity**: Explains religious diversity better than monotheism
3. **Theodicy**: No single all-powerful god to blame for evil

### Strongest Version

**Hindu Theology**: "Brahman (ultimate reality) manifests as many deities. Different paths to the divine for different temperaments."

### Main Challenges

1. **Parsimony**: Many gods less simple than one
2. **Fine-Tuning**: Who set physical constants if gods are limited?
3. **Moral Framework**: Competing gods, competing moralities

### Bayesian Priors

- **Simple version**: 1-5% (complexity penalty, weak evidence)

---

## 6. NATURALISM / ATHEISM (Physicalism)

### Core Claims

- **Only material/physical exists**
- **No God, no immaterial souls, no supernatural**
- **Universe explained by natural laws**

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Parsimony**: Simplest ontology (only matter/energy)
2. **Scientific Success**: Natural explanations consistently outperform supernatural
3. **Problem of Evil**: Suffering makes more sense if no loving God
4. **Divine Hiddenness**: If God existed and wanted relationship, He'd be more obvious

### Strongest Version

**Sean Carroll (Caltech physicist)**: "Naturalism + multiverse explains fine-tuning. Consciousness emerges from matter (no hard problem if we're patient). Morality is evolutionary/cultural. No need for God hypothesis."

### Main Challenges

1. **Fine-Tuning**: Without multiverse, constants seem designed
2. **Consciousness**: Hard problem—why is there subjective experience?
3. **Moral Realism**: Are objective moral values possible without God?
4. **Contingency**: Why does anything exist rather than nothing?

### Bayesian Priors

- **Bare naturalism** (no multiverse): 5-15% (fine-tuning problem severe)
- **Naturalism + multiverse**: 30-50% (anthropic explanation for fine-tuning)

## 7. AGNOSTICISM

### Core Claims

- **We don't know** if God exists
- **Maybe can't know** (strong agnosticism) or **don't currently know** (weak agnosticism)
- **Suspend judgment**

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Epistemic Humility**: Smart people disagree deeply—suggests truth is elusive
2. **Evidence Ambiguous**: Fine-tuning supports theism, evil supports atheism—wash
3. **Divine Hiddenness**: If God wanted us to know, He'd make it clearer

### Strongest Version

**Russell (Eric Buess, 2014-2015)**: "Weak agnostic weak atheist possibilian, with a big focus on the possibilian part. I lack positive belief in God (atheist), but acknowledge I don't know for certain (agnostic), and I'm open to possibilities (possibilian). Belief is involuntary—follows evidence assessment."

### Main Challenges

1. **Fence-Sitting**: Is agnosticism intellectually lazy? Or honest?
2. **Practical**: How do you live without taking a stance on ultimate questions?
3. **Bayesian**: Even agnostics have credences (say, 50/50)—not true suspension

### Bayesian Priors

- Credence for God's existence: **40-60%** (genuine uncertainty)

---

## 8. BUDDHISM (No-Self, Nirvana)

### Core Claims

- **No eternal soul** (anatman—no-self)
- **No creator God** (universe is beginningless, cyclical)
- **Suffering** caused by attachment/craving
- **Liberation** (nirvana) through Eightfold Path

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Empirical**: Meditation reveals no permanent self—only changing processes
2. **Theodicy**: No omnibenevolent God to reconcile with suffering
3. **Pragmatic**: Focus on reducing suffering here and now, not metaphysical speculation

### Strongest Version

**Dalai Lama**: "Buddhism is not a faith system but a science of mind. Testable through meditation. No need for creator God—universe is interdependent arising (pratītyasamutpāda)."

### Main Challenges

1. **Fine-Tuning**: Why is universe structured for life if no designer?
2. **Moral Realism**: Does karma provide objective morality, or is it just cause-and-effect?
3. **Consciousness**: If no-self, who experiences nirvana?

### Bayesian Priors

- **Simple version**: 5-15% (explains suffering well, but weak on fine-tuning)

---

## 9. SIMULATION HYPOTHESIS (Bostrom)

### Core Claims

- **We're in a computer simulation** created by advanced civilization
- **"God" = programmer**
- **Universe = software**

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Fine-Tuning**: Constants set by programmers
2. **Quantum Mechanics**: Discrete nature (Planck length) suggests pixelation
3. **Statistical**: If simulations possible, likely outnumber base reality (infinite simulations, one base)

### Strongest Version

**Nick Bostrom**: "At least one of these is true: (1) civilizations go extinct before creating simulations, (2) advanced civilizations choose not to create simulations, or (3) we're almost certainly in a simulation. If (1) and (2) are false, (3) follows."

### Main Challenges

1. **Unfalsifiable**: Can't test whether we're in simulation
2. **Regress**: Who created the programmers' universe?
3. **Theodicy**: Why would benevolent programmers allow suffering?

### Bayesian Priors

- **Simple version**: 5-20% (interesting but speculative, overlaps with theism)

---

## 10. THE ALIGNMENT HYPOTHESIS (Eric Buess, 2024)

### Core Claims

- **Universe is moral training environment** designed to solve AI alignment at cosmic scale
- **God exists** but uses suffering pedagogically to produce freely loving beings
- **Aligns with Christianity** but reframes theodicy through AI safety lens

### Best Arguments FOR

1. **Theodicy**: Suffering isn't gratuitous—it's necessary for genuine moral development (can't train aligned AI without real stakes)
2. **Fine-Tuning**: Constants set to allow moral agents to emerge
3. **Consciousness**: Hard problem solved if God creates immaterial souls
4. **Integration**: Synthesizes AI alignment, fine-tuning, consciousness, suffering in coherent framework

### Strongest Version

**Eric Buess**: "After a decade of doubt, I found belief possible again by asking different questions: Not 'Does God exist?' but 'What world do I want to live in? One where good God loves me and my kids, or one where He doesn't? Is belief possible, coherent, and plausible?' Alignment Hypothesis makes the 'square circle' rational—40-60% confidence, speculative but generative."

### Main Challenges

1. **Parsimony**: More complex than classical theism (violates Occam's Razor)
2. **Theodicy Gaps**: Doesn't fully resolve animal suffering or severe disabilities
3. **Christology**: How does Jesus' incarnation/atonement fit the framework?
4. **Speculation**: No way to verify we're in alignment training vs. base reality

### Bayesian Priors

- **Example credence range**: 40-60% (illustrative assessment, early beta v0.0.2)
- **General credence**: 10-30% (novel, speculative, but interesting)

## Summary: Steel-Manning Checklist

When presenting any worldview:

- [ ] **Present strongest version**: Not straw man, but how thoughtful proponent would argue
- [ ] **Cite best scholars**: Swinburne (theism), Carroll (naturalism), Dalai Lama (Buddhism), etc.
- [ ] **Acknowledge challenges**: Every view has weaknesses—be honest
- [ ] **Use Bayesian priors**: Reflect actual evidence, not biases
- [ ] **Compare fairly**: Don't privilege your own view—let evidence decide

**Goal**: Collaborative truth-seeking, not winning arguments.
