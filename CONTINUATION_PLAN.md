```markdown
**Hard Problem**: Why is there subjective experience (qualia)?

## Five Major Theories

### 1. IIT (Integrated Information Theory - Tononi)
- Consciousness = integrated information (Φ)
- Physical substrate irrelevant (any system with high Φ can be conscious)
- **Strength**: Mathematical framework
- **Weakness**: Counterintuitive (implies some simple systems conscious)

- **Strength**: Explains neural correlates
- **Weakness**: Doesn't explain WHY broadcasting creates qualia

- Quantum processes in microtubules generate consciousness
- **Strength**: Explains non-computational aspects
- **Weakness**: No evidence for quantum coherence in warm brain

### 4. Panpsychism
- Consciousness fundamental property of matter
- **Strength**: Avoids emergence problem
- **Weakness**: Combination problem (how do micro-consciousnesses combine?)

### 5. Illusionism (Dennett)
- Consciousness is illusion created by brain
- **Strength**: Dissolves hard problem
- **Weakness**: Counterintuitive (denies obvious subjective experience)
```

**Must Include**:

**Template Structure**:
```markdown
# Cognitive Biases in Theological Reasoning

## The Two Filters Framework (Russell's Method)

**Filter 1: Bias Recognition**
- What cognitive fallacies might affect this belief?
- Confirmation bias? Motivated reasoning? In-group bias?
- Am I reasoning toward truth or comfort?

**Filter 2: Evidential Testing**
- What testable predictions does this claim make?
- If true, what would we expect to observe?
- How does evidence compare to alternatives?

## Ten Major Biases

### 1. Confirmation Bias
**Definition**: Seeking evidence that confirms pre-existing beliefs
**Example**: Theist only reads apologetics, atheist only reads Dawkins
**Confidence Discount**: -10 to -20%
**How to Detect**: "Am I looking for truth or confirmation?"

### 2. Motivated Reasoning
**Definition**: Reasoning toward desired conclusion
**Example**: "I desperately want God to exist, so I'll accept weaker evidence"
**Confidence Discount**: -20 to -30%
**Russell's Quote**: "I want to believe and I admit that I'm primed for it"

### 3. Availability Heuristic
**Definition**: Overweighting vivid/recent examples
**Example**: 9/11 → all Muslims are terrorists
**Confidence Discount**: -10 to -15%

### 4. Anchoring Bias
**Definition**: Over-relying on first piece of information
**Example**: Raised Christian → prior for Christianity too high

### 5. In-Group Bias
**Definition**: Favoring my tribe's view
**Discount**: -10 to -15%

### 6. Dunning-Kruger Effect
**Definition**: Overconfidence due to lack of expertise
**Discount**: -15 to -25%

### 7. Belief Perseverance
**Definition**: Maintaining belief despite contradictory evidence

### 8. Backfire Effect
**Definition**: Contradictory evidence strengthens belief

### 9. Fundamental Attribution Error
**Definition**: Attributing others' actions to character, mine to circumstances

### 10. Sunk Cost Fallacy
**Definition**: "I've invested so much in this belief, can't abandon it now"

- "What do I want to be true?"
- "If I'm wrong, how would I know?"
- "Am I holding opposing views to same standard?"
- "Would I accept this evidence if it supported opposing view?"
```
