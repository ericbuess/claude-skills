# Cognitive Biases in Theological Reasoning

Guide to detecting and correcting biases using Russell's Two Filters Framework.

## The Two Filters Framework (Russell's Signature Method)

**Origin**: From Russell's (Eric's) blog russellandpascal.wordpress.com (2014-2015)

**Core Principle**: Subject **all beliefs** to two filters before accepting:

### Filter 1: Bias Recognition
**Question**: What cognitive fallacies might be affecting this belief?

**Check for**:
- Confirmation bias (seeking evidence that confirms)
- Motivated reasoning (reasoning toward desired conclusion)
- In-group bias (favoring my tribe's view)
- Availability heuristic (overweighting vivid examples)
- Am I reasoning toward **truth** or **comfort**?

### Filter 2: Evidential Testing
**Question**: What testable predictions does this claim make?

**Check for**:
- If true, what would we expect to observe?
- How does evidence compare to alternative explanations?
- Is this claim falsifiable? What would prove it wrong?
- Are we applying scientific methodology where applicable?

**Russell's Insight**: Most beliefs fail one or both filters. Rigorous thinking requires passing BOTH.

## Ten Major Cognitive Biases

### 1. Confirmation Bias

**Definition**: Seeking, interpreting, and remembering information that confirms pre-existing beliefs

**Theological Examples**:
- Theist only reads apologetics (William Lane Craig, C.S. Lewis)
- Atheist only reads New Atheists (Dawkins, Hitchens, Harris)
- Ignore

 contradictory evidence or interpret it away

**How to Detect**:
- "Am I looking for truth or confirmation?"
- "Have I seriously engaged strongest opposing arguments?"
- "Do I apply same standards to my view and opponents'?"

**Confidence Discount**: **-10 to -20%**

**Corrective**: Actively seek steel-manned opposing views. Read best scholars on other side.

**Russell's Example**: Read both Richard Swinburne (theist) and Graham Oppy (atheist) on fine-tuning.

---

### 2. Motivated Reasoning

**Definition**: Reasoning toward desired conclusion rather than following evidence

**Theological Examples**:
- "I desperately want God to exist, so I'll accept weaker evidence for theism"
- "I desperately don't want Hell to exist, so I'll reject testimonial evidence for afterlife"

**How to Detect**:
- "What do I want to be true?"
- "If this belief were false, would I be devastated?"
- "Am I reasoning toward truth or comfort?"

**Confidence Discount**: **-20 to -30%**

**Russell's Quote**: "I want to believe and I admit that I'm primed for it"

**Corrective**: If stakes are high (Heaven/Hell, meaning of life), reduce confidence proportionally. High stakes = high motivation = high bias risk.

---

### 3. Availability Heuristic

**Definition**: Overweighting vivid, recent, or emotionally salient examples

**Theological Examples**:
- 9/11 → "All Muslims are terrorists" (ignoring 1.8 billion peaceful Muslims)
- Answered prayer → "Prayer works" (ignoring unanswered prayers)
- Celebrity deconversion → "Christianity is false" (ignoring billions of believers)

**How to Detect**:
- "Am I overweighting this because it's vivid/recent?"
- "What's the base rate?" (How common is this actually?)

**Confidence Discount**: **-10 to -15%**

**Corrective**: Look for statistical data, not anecdotes.

---

### 4. Anchoring Bias

**Definition**: Over-relying on first piece of information encountered

**Theological Examples**:
- Raised Christian → prior for Christianity anchored too high
- First book read on topic sets frame for all subsequent reading

**How to Detect**:
- "Am I holding this view because it's what I was taught first?"
- "Would I believe this if raised in different culture?"

**Confidence Discount**: **-10%** if you haven't seriously questioned anchor

**Corrective**: Deliberately seek alternative starting points. Imagine you were born in Saudi Arabia (Islam), India (Hinduism), China (atheism/Buddhism). Would you still hold this belief?

---

### 5. In-Group Bias

**Definition**: Favoring beliefs held by my tribe/community

**Theological Examples**:
- Evangelical favors Reformed theology because their church teaches it
- Academic atheist favors naturalism because peers expect it
- Defensive when tribe's beliefs challenged

**How to Detect**:
- "Would I believe this if my community rejected it?"
- "Am I defending my tribe or defending truth?"

**Confidence Discount**: **-10 to -15%**

**Russell's Principle**: "Respect them now, because they are you in different circumstances"

**Corrective**: Practice epistemic humility. Recognize smart people in other tribes believe differently for reasons.

---

### 6. Dunning-Kruger Effect

**Definition**: Overconfidence due to lack of expertise

**Theological Examples**:
- Reading one apologetics book → "I understand cosmological argument perfectly"
- Watching YouTube debates → "I can refute Aquinas"
- Dismissing expert disagreement because "it's obvious to me"

**How to Detect**:
- "Have I studied this as deeply as experts?"
- "Do I understand strongest objections to my view?"

**Confidence Discount**: **-15 to -25%**

**Corrective**: When experts disagree, reduce confidence. If 50% of cosmologists accept multiverse, your 95% confidence against it is probably overconfident.

---

### 7. Belief Perseverance

**Definition**: Maintaining belief despite contradictory evidence

**Theological Examples**:
- "I believe Bible is inerrant despite contradictions—there must be harmonization I haven't seen"
- "I know evolution is false despite scientific consensus—scientists are biased"

**How to Detect**:
- "What evidence would change my mind?"
- "If I can't answer, I'm persevering unreasonably"

**Confidence Discount**: **-20%** if you can't specify falsification conditions

**Corrective**: Specify **concrete evidence** that would change your mind (like Russell's random number generator test).

---

### 8. Backfire Effect

**Definition**: Contradictory evidence strengthens belief (defensive reaction)

**Theological Examples**:
- Present biblical contradiction → "This strengthens my faith because Satan is attacking"
- Present scientific evidence against young earth → "Scientists are in conspiracy"

**How to Detect**:
- "Am I getting MORE certain when challenged?"
- "Is contradictory evidence making me defensive?"

**Confidence Discount**: **-20 to -30%**

**Corrective**: Pause when defensive. "Why am I reacting emotionally? What's the steel-manned version of this objection?"

---

### 9. Fundamental Attribution Error

**Definition**: Attributing others' actions to character, mine to circumstances

**Theological Examples**:
- "I believe because I've carefully studied evidence" (circumstances: intelligence, access)
- "They believe because they're indoctrinated" (character flaw)

**How to Detect**:
- "If I were born in their circumstances, would I believe as they do?"

**Russell's Quote**: "If you were born in their environment with their DNA and shared their exact experiences, you might believe exactly as they do"

**Corrective**: Practice empathy. Recognize beliefs are largely circumstantial.

---

### 10. Sunk Cost Fallacy

**Definition**: "I've invested so much in this belief, I can't abandon it now"

**Theological Examples**:
- "I've spent 20 years as Christian, can't question now"
- "I've defended atheism publicly, can't admit I'm uncertain"
- "I've lost friends over this belief, can't let that be for nothing"

**How to Detect**:
- "Am I holding this belief because of past investment?"
- "Would I adopt this belief if starting fresh today?"

**Confidence Discount**: **-15 to -20%**

**Corrective**: Past investment is irrelevant to truth. "Most of us ultimately care more about truth than about being right the first time" (Russell).

## Metacognitive Prompts

Use these questions to audit your thinking:

1. **Desire Check**: "What do I want to be true about this?"
2. **Falsifiability**: "What evidence would change my mind?"
3. **Perspective Shift**: "If I were born in different culture/family, would I still believe this?"
4. **Double Standard**: "Am I holding opposing views to same evidential standard?"
5. **Acceptance Test**: "Would I accept this evidence if it supported opposing view?"
6. **Steel-Man**: "Can I present opposing view so compellingly that proponent would agree?"
7. **Expert Check**: "Do qualified experts disagree? If so, am I overconfident?"
8. **Emotional Audit**: "Am I reasoning toward truth or toward comfort?"

## Expert Disagreement Adjustments

(From bayesian-reasoning.md)

When qualified experts disagree significantly:

- **Modest disagreement** (60/40 split): Reduce confidence by **-10%**
- **Significant disagreement** (70/30 split): Reduce confidence by **-20%**
- **Deep disagreement** (80/20 or worse split): Reduce confidence by **-30%**

**Example**: Fine-Tuning
- You calculate P(Theism | Fine-Tuning) = 70%
- BUT cosmologists deeply disagree on multiverse (50/50 split)
- **Adjusted confidence**: 70% - 20% = **50%**

---

## Cumulative Bias Discounts

If multiple biases detected, **compound discounts**:

**Example**: Evaluating Theism
- Start: 70% confidence
- Motivated reasoning detected (desperately want God to exist): -25% → 45%
- In-group bias (all my friends are Christian): -12% → 33%
- Confirmation bias (only read apologetics): -15% → 18%
- **Final adjusted confidence**: **18%** (much lower than initial 70%)

**Lesson**: Biases compound. Rigor requires identifying ALL biases, not just one.

## The Two Filters in Practice

**Example Question**: "Is the Bible inerrant?"

### Filter 1: Bias Recognition

**Check**:
- Confirmation bias? (Only reading inerrancy defenders?)
- Motivated reasoning? (Desperately need Bible to be perfect?)
- In-group bias? (Church teaches inerrancy?)
- Anchoring? (Raised to believe it?)

**Likely biases**: Motivated reasoning (-25%), In-group bias (-12%), Anchoring (-10%)

**Starting confidence**: 80%
**After Bias Filter**: 80% - 47% = **33%**

### Filter 2: Evidential Testing

**Testable Predictions** if Bible inerrant:
- No historical errors
- No contradictions
- No scientific errors

**Evidence**:
- Genealogies of Jesus (Matthew vs. Luke) don't match
- Creation accounts (Genesis 1 vs. 2) differ
- Historical: Census of Quirinius timing doesn't align with Herod's death

**Likelihood**: P(These contradictions | Inerrancy) = 5-15%

**Posterior**: 33% × 0.10 ≈ **3-5% confidence in inerrancy**

**Conclusion**: After both filters, inerrancy is untenable. But "Bible is valuable despite errors" remains viable (80% confidence).

## Summary Checklist

For every theological claim:

- [ ] **Two Filters Applied**:
  - [ ] Filter 1: What biases might affect this?
  - [ ] Filter 2: What testable predictions? What's the evidence?
- [ ] **Bias Audit**: Checked for all 10 biases
- [ ] **Confidence Discounts**: Applied cumulative discounts
- [ ] **Expert Disagreement**: Adjusted for disagreement
- [ ] **Metacognitive Prompts**: Asked at least 3 prompts
- [ ] **Falsifiability**: Specified what would change mind
- [ ] **Final Confidence**: Stated as range (not point estimate)
