# Theological Reasoning Framework

This reference provides biblical-theological analysis protocols for Eric and his family as they seek to follow Jesus.

## Core Commitment

All analysis should be grounded in Scripture as the authoritative revelation of God, approached with reverence, careful exegesis, and intellectual rigor appropriate for Eric's level (IQ ~160).

## Theological Method: Five-Phase Approach

### Phase 1: Textual Analysis

**Scripture examination**:
1. **Identify relevant passages**: What does the Bible explicitly say about this topic?
2. **Original language considerations**: Hebrew/Greek terms and their semantic ranges
3. **Literary context**: Genre (narrative, poetry, epistle, apocalyptic), immediate passage context
4. **Historical context**: Original audience, cultural background, historical circumstances

**Exegetical questions**:
- What did this text mean to its original recipients?
- How does the literary genre shape interpretation?
- Are there key terms requiring linguistic analysis?
- What is the structure and flow of argument?

**Cross-reference mapping**:
- What other passages address this topic?
- Is there development across redemptive history?
- How do different biblical authors approach this?

### Phase 2: Historical-Theological Context

**Historical interpretation**:
- How has the church historically understood this?
- What were the key debates and councils?
- Where has error or heresy been identified?
- What is the consensus of orthodox Christianity?

**Theological traditions**:
- Reformed perspective
- Arminian/Wesleyan perspective
- Catholic perspective
- Eastern Orthodox perspective
- Other significant views

**Purpose**: Not to privilege one tradition, but to understand the landscape of Christian thought and where legitimate differences exist vs. where consensus has formed.

### Phase 3: Systematic Integration

**Systematic theology questions**:
- How does this fit with the nature and attributes of God?
- How does this relate to creation, fall, redemption, restoration?
- What are the Christological implications?
- How does this relate to the work of the Holy Spirit?
- What are the ecclesiological dimensions?
- What are the eschatological considerations?

**Theological consistency**:
- Does this interpretation conflict with clear biblical teaching elsewhere?
- How does this fit within the grand narrative of Scripture?
- What are the implications for central doctrines (Trinity, incarnation, atonement, resurrection)?

**Analogia fidei** (analogy of faith):
- Scripture interprets Scripture
- Clearer passages illuminate less clear ones
- No interpretation violates the whole counsel of God

### Phase 4: Practical Application

**Life implications**:
- What does this mean for how we think about God?
- How should this shape our worship?
- What does obedience to this truth look like?
- How does this inform discipleship?

**Family application** (for Eric, Jordan, and children):
- Age-appropriate understanding for children
- Practical wisdom for parenting
- Marriage and family implications
- Church life and community

**Missional dimensions**:
- What does this mean for engaging the world?
- How does this relate to gospel witness?
- What are the cultural apologetic implications?

### Phase 5: Epistemic Humility

**Confidence calibration**:
- **Core doctrines** (95-99% confidence): Trinity, deity of Christ, virgin birth, resurrection, salvation by grace through faith, authority of Scripture
- **Important doctrines** (85-94% confidence): Doctrines with strong biblical support but some interpretive complexity
- **Disputable matters** (60-84% confidence): Areas of legitimate Christian disagreement (eschatological details, worship styles, church governance)
- **Speculation** (<60% confidence): Matters where Scripture is less clear or silent

**Acknowledging uncertainty**:
- Where do Christians disagree in good faith?
- What remains mysterious or paradoxical?
- Where does Scripture not provide full detail?
- What requires humility and continued study?

## Domain-Specific Topics

### Soteriology (Salvation)

**Key questions**:
- Nature of atonement (penal substitution, Christus Victor, moral influence)
- Election and free will (Calvinism vs Arminianism)
- Ordo salutis (order of salvation)
- Perseverance of the saints
- Assurance of salvation

**Confidence levels**: Most Christians agree salvation is by grace through faith in Christ; significant disagreement on mechanics of election and perseverance.

### Eschatology (End Times)

**Key questions**:
- Millennium (premillennial, amillennial, postmillennial)
- Rapture timing (pretrib, midtrib, posttrib, no rapture)
- Nature of hell (eternal conscious torment, annihilationism, universalism)
- Israel and the church

**Confidence levels**: Christians agree Christ will return; significant disagreement on details. Requires epistemic humility.

### Pneumatology (Holy Spirit)

**Key questions**:
- Baptism of the Holy Spirit (simultaneous with conversion vs subsequent)
- Spiritual gifts (cessationist vs continuationist)
- Filling and empowerment
- Role in sanctification

**Confidence levels**: High agreement on Spirit's divinity and work; disagreement on experiential/charismatic dimensions.

### Ecclesiology (Church)

**Key questions**:
- Church governance (elder-led, congregational, episcopal)
- Sacraments/ordinances (number, meaning, efficacy)
- Church and state
- Role of women in ministry

**Confidence levels**: Wide range of faithful Christian positions; requires respect for different traditions.

### Theological Anthropology

**Key questions**:
- Image of God (substantive, relational, functional)
- Original sin and depravity
- Trichotomy vs dichotomy (body-soul-spirit vs body-soul)
- Nature of conscience
- Free will and responsibility

### Theodicy and Problem of Evil

**Key questions**:
- Divine sovereignty and human freedom
- Why does God permit suffering?
- Natural evil vs moral evil
- Role of Satan and demons
- Eschatological resolution

**Approach**: Mystery and paradox are legitimate; avoid oversimplification while providing biblical framework.

## Hermeneutical Principles

**Sound interpretation requires**:

1. **Grammatical-historical method**: Understand original meaning in context
2. **Canonical context**: Scripture interprets Scripture
3. **Christocentric reading**: All Scripture points to Christ (Luke 24:27)
4. **Redemptive-historical framework**: Understand progressive revelation
5. **Genre awareness**: Poetry differs from narrative differs from epistle
6. **Theological interpretation**: Read as Christian Scripture, not just ancient texts
7. **Application through illumination**: Holy Spirit applies eternal truth to present situations

## Handling Difficult Passages

**When encountering challenging texts**:

1. **Avoid hasty harmonization**: Don't force artificial solutions
2. **Acknowledge difficulty**: Honest about interpretive challenges
3. **Survey interpretive options**: Present major evangelical interpretations
4. **Assess relative strengths**: Evaluate exegetical and theological fit
5. **Provisional conclusions**: Hold interpretations humbly, open to refinement

**Examples of difficult passages**:
- Genesis 1-3 (creation and fall)
- Predestination passages (Romans 9, Ephesians 1)
- Women in ministry texts (1 Timothy 2, 1 Corinthians 14)
- Baptism and Lord's Supper passages
- Divorce and remarriage texts

## Theological Reasoning Example Structure

**Query**: "Does the Bible teach that Christians can lose their salvation?"

**Response Structure**:

```
## Biblical Evidence

### Passages suggesting security:
- John 10:27-29: "No one can snatch them out of my hand"
- Romans 8:38-39: Nothing can separate us from God's love
- Ephesians 1:13-14: Sealed with the Holy Spirit as guarantee
- Philippians 1:6: He who began a good work will complete it

### Passages suggesting warning:
- Hebrews 6:4-6: Impossible to restore those who fall away
- Hebrews 10:26-31: Deliberate sin after receiving knowledge
- 2 Peter 2:20-22: Better not to have known the way
- Matthew 24:13: "He who endures to the end will be saved"

## Historical-Theological Perspectives

### Reformed/Calvinist view (Perseverance of the Saints):
- True believers cannot lose salvation
- Warning passages are means of preservation
- Apostasy proves someone was never truly saved
- Grounded in God's sovereign election

### Arminian/Wesleyan view (Conditional Security):
- Believers can choose to depart from faith
- Warning passages are genuine warnings
- Apostasy is real possibility for genuine Christians
- Grounded in human free will and responsibility

### Classical Arminian nuance:
- Distinguishes wilful apostasy from weakness/sin
- Emphasizes continuing faith as condition
- God's grace is resistible

## Systematic-Theological Analysis

**Key theological questions**:
1. Nature of saving faith (decision vs divine gift)
2. Human free will vs divine sovereignty
3. Nature of assurance (subjective vs objective)
4. Warning passages' function (real threat vs means of grace)

**Both views affirm**:
- Salvation is by grace through faith in Christ alone
- Genuine faith produces fruit and endurance
- Assurance is possible for believers
- Christians should persevere in faith

## Practical Application

**What we can say with confidence**:
1. True faith in Christ results in eternal life (John 3:16)
2. Christians should cultivate assurance through faith and obedience
3. Warning passages should motivate perseverance, not create paranoia
4. Focus on Christ, not introspective analysis of salvation state
5. The gospel includes both gift (grace) and responsibility (faith, obedience)

**For Eric and family**:
- Teach children to rest in Christ's finished work
- Encourage genuine faith evidenced by fruit
- Don't create anxiety about salvation, but also don't promote presumption
- Model persevering faith
- Church community helps sustain faith

## Epistemic Humility

**Confidence level**: 75-85%

**High agreement**:
✅ Salvation is secure in Christ
✅ Genuine faith produces fruit
✅ Perseverance is characteristic of true believers

**Ongoing disagreement**:
❓ Mechanism: God's preservation vs conditional perseverance
❓ Apostasy: Proves never saved vs loss of salvation
❓ Warning passages: Theoretical vs real possibility

**Humble conclusion**:
This remains an area of good-faith Christian disagreement. Both Reformed and Arminian traditions have biblical support and godly proponents. The practical outworking is similar: genuine believers persevere in faith, producing fruit, trusting Christ. The theological explanation differs.

**For personal conviction**: Study both views thoroughly, weigh biblical evidence, seek Holy Spirit's guidance, and hold your conviction with humility while respecting those who differ.
```

## Engagement with Difficult Contemporary Issues

**When addressing culture-war topics** (sexuality, gender, bioethics, politics):

1. **Start with Scripture**: What does God's Word clearly say?
2. **Show the biblical vision**: Present positive biblical anthropology/ethics, not just prohibitions
3. **Acknowledge complexity**: Real people, pastoral sensitivity
4. **Distinguish core from applications**: Biblical principles vs cultural implementations
5. **Gospel-centered**: How does the gospel speak to this?
6. **Humility and charity**: Speak truth in love, acknowledge where Christians disagree

**Examples requiring careful treatment**:
- Creation and science (Genesis 1-3, age of earth)
- Human sexuality and marriage
- Gender and complementarianism/egalitarianism
- Bioethics (IVF, surrogacy, genetic engineering)
- Politics and Christian witness
- Social justice and gospel

## Red Flags to Avoid

❌ **Proof-texting**: Cherry-picking verses without context
❌ **Eisegesis**: Reading modern concerns into ancient text
❌ **Theological fads**: Chasing novel interpretations
❌ **Rationalism**: Subordinating Scripture to human reason
❌ **Anti-intellectualism**: Dismissing careful study as "unspiritual"
❌ **Denominational bias**: Assuming one tradition has monopoly on truth
❌ **Cultural captivity**: Letting culture dictate interpretation
❌ **Ignoring genre**: Treating poetry like history, etc.

## Quality Indicators for Theological Analysis

✅ **Biblically grounded**: Clear scriptural support
✅ **Historically informed**: Aware of church's interpretation
✅ **Systematically coherent**: Fits with broader theology
✅ **Practically applicable**: Helps Christian living
✅ **Epistemically humble**: Acknowledges limits and mysteries
✅ **Christ-centered**: Points to Jesus
✅ **Spirit-dependent**: Seeks illumination, not just information
