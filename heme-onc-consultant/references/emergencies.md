# Oncologic Emergencies Reference

Quick reference for time-sensitive hematology/oncology emergencies requiring immediate recognition and intervention.

## Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS)

### Risk Factors
**High Risk:**
- High-grade lymphoma (Burkitt, lymphoblastic)
- Acute leukemia with WBC >100k
- Bulky disease (>10cm mass or LDH >2x ULN)
- Pre-existing renal dysfunction

**Moderate Risk:**
- Intermediate/high-grade lymphomas
- AML, especially with WBC >25k
- CLL treated with venetoclax
- Solid tumors with rapid response (germ cell, SCLC)

### Laboratory Criteria (Cairo-Bishop)
Two or more within 3 days before or 7 days after cytotoxic therapy:
- Uric acid ≥8 mg/dL or 25% increase
- Potassium ≥6 mEq/L or 25% increase  
- Phosphate ≥4.5 mg/dL (adults) or 25% increase
- Calcium ≤7 mg/dL or 25% decrease

### Management
**Prevention (High Risk):**
1. Rasburicase 0.2 mg/kg IV x 1-2 doses (preferred if high risk)
2. Allopurinol 300-600 mg PO if lower risk
3. Aggressive hydration: 200-300 mL/hr (3-4 L/day)
4. Monitor: Chem-10, LDH q6-12h for 48-72h
5. Avoid nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, contrast)
6. NO potassium or phosphate in IV fluids

**Treatment:**
- Rasburicase 0.2 mg/kg (repeat if uric acid >7)
- Hemodialysis for: K+ >6, Cr >10, symptomatic hypocalcemia, refractory hyperphosphatemia
- Correct electrolytes carefully (watch QT interval)

## Febrile Neutropenia

### Definitions
- Fever: Single temp ≥38.3°C (101°F) OR ≥38°C (100.4°F) for ≥1 hour
- Neutropenia: ANC <500 or <1000 with expected decline to <500

### Risk Stratification
**High Risk (requires admission):**
- Profound neutropenia (ANC <100)
- Expected prolonged neutropenia (>7 days)
- Hemodynamic instability
- Pneumonia, hypoxia
- Liver dysfunction (ALT >5x ULN)
- Renal dysfunction (Cr >2.0)
- MASCC score <21

**Low Risk (may consider outpatient):**
- ANC 100-500
- Expected brief neutropenia (<7 days)
- No organ dysfunction
- MASCC score ≥21
- Able to take oral meds
- Good social support

### Initial Management
**Within 1 Hour:**
1. Blood cultures (2 sets from separate sites)
2. CBC with differential
3. CMP (renal, hepatic function)
4. Lactate
5. Chest X-ray
6. Urinalysis and culture
7. Any symptomatic site cultures

**Empiric Antibiotics (within 1 hour):**
- **Monotherapy**: Cefepime 2g IV q8h OR piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6h
- **Add vancomycin** if: 
  * Skin/soft tissue infection
  * Hemodynamic instability
  * Pneumonia
  * Blood culture gram-positive cocci
  * Mucositis with suspected line infection
  * MRSA colonization
  
**Antifungal Coverage:**
- Not routine initially
- Consider if: persistent fever >4-7 days on antibiotics, prior fungal infection, colonization

### Escalation
**Persistent Fever at 72h:**
- Reassess clinically
- Repeat cultures
- Consider chest CT for occult pneumonia
- Add antifungal: voriconazole or liposomal amphotericin B

**Septic Shock:**
- STAT ID consultation
- Broaden coverage: meropenem + vancomycin ± antifungal
- Source control if indicated
- ICU transfer

## Hypercalcemia of Malignancy

### Severity
- Mild: 10.5-12 mg/dL (often asymptomatic)
- Moderate: 12-14 mg/dL (symptoms present)
- Severe: >14 mg/dL (life-threatening)

### Symptoms
- Neurologic: Confusion, lethargy, coma
- GI: Nausea, constipation
- Renal: Polyuria, dehydration, AKI
- Cardiac: Shortened QT interval, arrhythmias

### Management
**Immediate (All Patients):**
1. IV hydration: 200-300 mL/hr NS (3-4 L/day if tolerated)
2. Monitor: Calcium q6-12h, CMP, I/O, volume status

**Bisphosphonate (Corrected Ca >12):**
- Zoledronic acid 4 mg IV over 15 min (single dose)
- OR Pamidronate 60-90 mg IV over 2-4 hours
- Effect: 2-4 days to peak, lasts 2-4 weeks
- Adjust for renal function (avoid if Cr >3-4)

**Denosumab (If bisphosphonate fails or contraindicated):**
- 120 mg SC weekly x 4 doses, then monthly
- Does not require renal dose adjustment
- Give calcium/vitamin D supplementation

**Adjunctive (Severe, Cr >12-14):**
- Calcitonin 4 units/kg SC/IM q12h (rapid but temporary)
- Consider dialysis if refractory and life-threatening

**Treat Underlying Malignancy:**
- Many causes: myeloma, breast, lung, RCC, lymphoma
- PTHrP-mediated vs osteolytic bone disease
- May need systemic therapy or radiation to bone lesions

## Spinal Cord Compression

### Presentation
- Back pain (90%) - focal, worse with recumbency, percussion tenderness
- Motor weakness (75%) - ascending from legs
- Sensory changes (50%) - sensory level, numbness
- Autonomic dysfunction (50%) - urinary retention, bowel incontinence

### Diagnosis
- MRI entire spine (preferred) within hours
- If MRI contraindicated: CT myelography
- Plain films inadequate

### Immediate Management (STAT)
**Within Hours:**
1. Dexamethasone 10 mg IV STAT, then 4 mg IV q6h
   - If definite compression and neurologic deficit: consider 100 mg load
2. STAT radiation oncology and neurosurgery consults
3. Keep patient flat (minimize movement until imaging)
4. MRI entire spine (multiple levels in 10-30%)
5. Bladder catheter if retention

**Definitive Treatment:**
- Radiation therapy (most cases): 30 Gy in 10 fractions OR 8 Gy x 1
- Surgery (decompression + stabilization) if:
  * Spinal instability
  * Unknown primary needing tissue
  * Radioresistant tumor
  * Prior RT to site
  * Progression through RT
  * Neurologic deterioration during RT

**Prognosis:**
- Ambulatory at diagnosis → 80% remain ambulatory
- Paraplegic <24h → 30% walk
- Paraplegic >48h → <10% walk
- **Time is tissue - rapid treatment critical**

## Superior Vena Cava (SVC) Syndrome

### Causes
- Lung cancer (70%) - SCLC, NSCLC
- Lymphoma (15%)
- Thrombus (catheter-related)
- Mediastinal mass (thymoma, germ cell tumor)

### Presentation
- Facial/arm swelling
- Dyspnea (especially when supine)
- Chest pain
- Cough, hoarseness
- Headache, dizziness
- Facial plethora, venous distension

### Diagnosis
- CT chest with IV contrast (define anatomy, thrombus)
- If no tissue diagnosis: biopsy (endobronchial, mediastinoscopy, CT-guided)
- Avoid general anesthesia if severe (airway compression risk)

### Management
**Supportive:**
- Elevate head of bed
- Oxygen if hypoxic
- Diuretics if fluid overloaded

**Anticoagulation (if thrombus):**
- Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h OR apixaban/rivaroxaban
- Remove catheter if catheter-associated
- 3-6 months anticoagulation

**Steroids:**
- Dexamethasone 4 mg IV q6h (if lymphoma suspected or severe)
- NOT if need biopsy for diagnosis (may interfere)

**Definitive:**
- Chemotherapy/radiation based on tumor type
  * SCLC: chemo (rapid response)
  * NSCLC: RT ± chemo
  * Lymphoma: chemo (often dramatic response)
- Endovascular stenting if:
  * Life-threatening symptoms
  * Failed medical therapy
  * Thrombus despite anticoagulation
  * Need rapid symptom relief before systemic therapy

**True Emergency?**
- Controversial - historically considered emergency but usually NOT
- Emergent treatment needed only if:
  * Airway compromise
  * Cerebral edema
  * Laryngeal edema
- Most cases can wait 24-48h for diagnosis before treatment

## Hyperviscosity Syndrome

### Causes
- Waldenström macroglobulinemia (IgM, most common)
- Multiple myeloma (IgG, IgA - less common)
- Leukostasis (WBC >100k, especially AML M4/M5)

### Classic Triad
1. **Bleeding**: Mucosal oozing, epistaxis, GI bleeding
2. **Neurologic**: Headache, confusion, vision changes, stroke, seizures
3. **Cardiac**: CHF, dyspnea, pulmonary edema

### Diagnosis
- Serum viscosity >4 cP (normal 1.4-1.8)
- Fundoscopy: "sausage-link" retinal veins, hemorrhages, papilledema
- Protein gap (total protein >> albumin + globulin suggests paraprotein)
- SPEP/UPEP/IFE, quantitative immunoglobulins
- Peripheral smear (rouleaux, if leukostasis: blasts)

### Management
**Immediate:**
1. **Plasmapheresis** (treatment of choice for IgM)
   - 1-1.5 plasma volumes daily until symptoms improve
   - Usually 3-7 days
   - Rapid symptom relief (within hours)
   
2. **Hydration** (maintain euvolemia - avoid overload)

3. **NO TRANSFUSIONS** if possible (increases viscosity)
   - If absolutely needed: small volumes, with plasmapheresis

**Definitive:**
- Treat underlying disease:
  * Waldenström: rituximab-based (avoid plasmapheresis if rituximab alone)
  * Myeloma: bortezomib-based induction
- Chronic plasmapheresis until disease controlled

## Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)

### Causes in Oncology
- **APL** (most important) - "low-grade" DIC
- Sepsis (common)
- Metastatic adenocarcinoma (especially mucinous)
- AML (other subtypes less common than APL)

### Lab Criteria (ISTH)
- Platelet count <100k (or dropping)
- Elevated fibrin markers (D-dimer, fibrin split products)
- Prolonged PT (>3 sec above normal or INR >1.4)
- Fibrinogen <100 mg/dL

### APL-Specific DIC
- Occurs at presentation (before/during induction)
- "Consumptive coagulopathy"
- Exacerbated by differentiation syndrome

### Management
**General DIC:**
1. **Treat underlying cause** (most important)
2. Transfusion support:
   - PLT if <10k (prophylactic) or <50k if bleeding
   - FFP if bleeding + prolonged PT (10-15 mL/kg)
   - Cryoprecipitate if fibrinogen <100 (goal >150)
3. Consider heparin only if thrombosis predominant

**APL-Specific:**
1. **ATRA** 45 mg/m² PO divided BID (START IMMEDIATELY)
2. **Arsenic trioxide** 0.15 mg/kg IV daily (often combined)
3. **Aggressive transfusion support:**
   - PLT >30-50k
   - Fibrinogen >150
   - Transfuse even if counts "adequate" - rapid consumption
4. **Monitor coags q12h** during induction
5. **Delay anthracycline** until coags improving if possible
6. **Watch for differentiation syndrome**

## Neutropenic Enterocolitis (Typhlitis)

### Presentation
- Right lower quadrant pain (cecal inflammation)
- Fever, diarrhea (may be bloody)
- Nausea, vomiting, abdominal distension
- Usually ANC <500

### Diagnosis
- CT abdomen/pelvis: bowel wall thickening (cecum >10mm), pneumatosis, free fluid
- Surgery consult early

### Management
**Medical (Most Cases):**
1. Broad-spectrum antibiotics
   - Meropenem 1g IV q8h OR piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6h
   - PLUS metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h
   - PLUS vancomycin if septic or prior MRSA
2. Bowel rest (NPO, TPN if prolonged)
3. NG tube if obstruction/ileus
4. Serial exams q4-6h
5. G-CSF (consider if not already on)

**Surgical (Urgent):**
- Free air (perforation)
- Clinical deterioration despite antibiotics
- Uncontrolled bleeding
- Absolute: peritonitis, free perforation
- Relative: bowel wall >10mm, ongoing symptoms >5 days

**Prognosis:**
- Mortality 30-50% if surgery required
- Medical management successful in 50-70%

## Key Emergency Principles

1. **Time-critical recognition**: Delays worsen outcomes dramatically
2. **Simultaneous workup and treatment**: Don't wait for confirmatory tests
3. **Early specialist consultation**: Involve oncology, surgery, ID, ICU early
4. **Treat underlying malignancy**: Most effective definitive treatment
5. **Know your resources**: Is plasmapheresis available? When can RT start?
